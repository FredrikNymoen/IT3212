\subsection{Contour Detection}
\label{sec:contour-detection}

\subsubsection{Apply contour detection to the same dataset}
The blob detector represents local contrast with circles. Contour detection
was used to examine the boundaries of intensity regions instead, using the
same preprocessing. This analysis covers the ten fixed examples introduced
in the blob section. The blob baseline was recomputed on these same inputs
so that differences between methods are not caused by different image samples.
All contour statistics and parameter experiments concern these ten images;
the conclusions do not extend to a full-dataset contour evaluation.

The baseline first smooths normalized grayscale intensities with a Gaussian
filter ($\sigma=1$ pixel), then forms the binary mask in
Equation~\eqref{eq:contour-mask}:
\begin{equation}
 M(x,y)=\begin{cases}1,&(G_1*I)(x,y)>0.5,\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
 \label{eq:contour-mask}
\end{equation}
ContourPy's serial marching-squares engine traces the mask's 0.5 level,
interpolating boundary positions between pixel centers. The implementation
retains closed loops of area at least 25 square pixels and records excluded
open and small paths. Open paths are not artificially closed across the frame.
Outer boundaries and boundaries around holes are counted separately; the
algorithm does not build an object hierarchy. Consequently, a contour is a
boundary of a mask region, not necessarily a complete vehicle outline.

Geometry checks verified a square's known area, perimeter, and centroid,
extraction of a binary rectangle, exclusion of a frame-crossing open path,
separate inner/outer ring boundaries, and no detections on a constant image.

\subsubsection{Calculate counts, areas, and perimeters}
For ordered vertices $(x_i,y_i)$ of a closed loop, with vertex $n+1$ equal to
vertex 1, Equation~\eqref{eq:contour-geometry} defines the area $A$ and
perimeter $P$:
\begin{equation}
 A=\frac12\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i y_{i+1}-x_{i+1}y_i)\right|,
 \qquad
 P=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sqrt{(x_{i+1}-x_i)^2+(y_{i+1}-y_i)^2}.
 \label{eq:contour-geometry}
\end{equation}
Area is measured in processed square pixels and perimeter in processed pixels.
The baseline retains 158 contours (7--23 per image), with a contour-weighted
mean area of 349.7 square pixels and mean perimeter of 95.1 pixels.
Table~\ref{tab:detection-image-stats} gives every analyzed image's count and
mean measurements. Individual loop areas, perimeters, and centroids are
exported in \texttt{baseline\_contours.csv}. Boundary vertices are saved
separately in the contour-results directory.

\subsubsection{Compare blob and contour detection}
For the same ten images, the two baselines produce 695 blobs and 158 contours.
This matched comparison excludes the remaining images from the full-dataset
blob analysis.
The larger blob count reflects a different feature definition, not greater
accuracy. LoG summarizes local contrast using centers and scale estimates;
contours follow the irregular boundaries of thresholded regions.

In \texttt{pickup/PIC\_0.jpg}, there are 110 blobs but 14 contours
(Figures~\ref{fig:blob-example} and~\ref{fig:contour-parameters}). Repeated
circles follow window and bumper details, whereas individual contours can
describe elongated window and bumper regions. Neither result identifies the
whole pickup. A blob's $\pi r^2$ is a scale-based circular approximation,
whereas contour area measures an enclosed polygon; these are not interchangeable
estimates of the same object. Likewise, the contour brightness threshold of
0.5 and LoG response threshold of 0.15 operate on different quantities.

\subsubsection{Discuss advantages and limitations}
LoG provides compact center-and-scale features and detects both bright and
dark regions without choosing one global foreground brightness class. This
representation provides approximate location and scale without tracing a
boundary. A circle does not describe the outline of an elongated region, and edges,
reflections, and background texture can generate multiple detections.
The scale range and frame exclusion also restrict what can be found.

Contours preserve irregular boundary geometry and support area and perimeter
measurements. The measured boundaries depend on the segmentation: a global
brightness threshold can merge unrelated regions or fragment one object.
Discarding open paths removes frame-intersecting boundaries, and separate
hole loops are not equivalent to separate objects. Illumination, perspective,
occlusion, and resolution affect both approaches. Neither method uses the
vehicle labels to recognize objects, and without boundary or blob annotations
detection precision and recall cannot be calculated. The ten-image comparison
also cannot establish performance across the full dataset.

\subsubsection{Analyze threshold and filter-size effects}
The dependence on segmentation was examined by varying brightness threshold,
smoothing, and minimum retained area. Each variant changes one baseline
parameter on all ten images. Table~\ref{tab:contour-parameters} reports the
aggregate changes, while Figure~\ref{fig:contour-parameters} shows their effect
on the pickup boundaries. The threshold changes which pixels enter the mask;
smoothing changes local intensity variation; minimum area filters the resulting
loops without changing their boundaries.

\textbf{Brightness threshold.} Thresholds 0.35, 0.5, and 0.65 produce
182, 158, and 109 contours across the sample. The pickup counts are 14, 14,
and 11. Lowering the threshold changes the window, bumper,
and plate boundaries without changing the first two counts. At the high
threshold, the broad window region is replaced by a narrow highlight.
Thus, equal counts can conceal substantial changes in shape and position.
Thresholding alters connectivity, so its effect on count is not generally
monotonic even though the sample totals decrease here.

\textbf{Smoothing.} Removing smoothing increases retained contours to 230
and mean perimeter from 95.1 to 101.8 pixels. It produces 1,933 discarded small
or degenerate loops, compared with 436 at baseline. The pickup has 27 retained
contours with visibly more fragmented and jagged boundaries. Increasing
$\sigma$ to 2 reduces the sample total to 96 and mean perimeter to 88.1 pixels.
Small details disappear or merge, but the 13 pickup contours are not simply a
subset of the baseline: smoothing can also change connectivity.

\textbf{Minimum area.} Increasing the cutoff from 25 to 100 square pixels
reduces the total from 158 to 65, while mean retained area rises from 349.7
to 773.4 square pixels. This leaves the mask and surviving boundaries
unchanged; it filters measurements rather than improving segmentation.
The pickup retains 10 of its 14 baseline loops.

These experiments show a trade-off between retaining fine detail and
suppressing fragmented regions. They do not establish an optimal setting;
that requires a target definition and annotated evaluation data.

\subsubsection{Give examples of suitable applications}
\textbf{Compact contrast features.} Candidate lamp details, reflective spots,
or dark patches can be represented by a center
and approximate scale. Figure~\ref{fig:blob-example} illustrates such local responses in the pickup,
although a subsequent classifier or validation step would be needed to identify
which responses actually belong to a desired vehicle component.

\textbf{Shape measurements.} The sedan window region in
Figure~\ref{fig:contour-example} has an irregular outline that is not
represented by a single circle. When segmentation isolates the intended region, contours permit
direct area and perimeter measurements. A controlled background with a brightness range distinct from the object
would allow global thresholding to separate the silhouette. The present
photographs contain shadows, reflections, and background regions that overlap
the vehicle's brightness range.

For whole-vehicle recognition in these scenes, neither baseline
establishes one detection or one complete outline per vehicle. The results
support choosing a method according to the required representation: local
center/scale features or the geometry of adequately segmented boundaries.
