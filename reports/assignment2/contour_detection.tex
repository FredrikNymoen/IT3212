\subsection{Contour Detection}
\label{sec:contour-detection}

\subsubsection{Apply contour detection to the same dataset}
A contour follows a region's boundary. The baseline, all parameter experiments,
and the matched blob comparison cover all 1,310 images. Both methods use
identical prepared inputs.

The grayscale image $I$ is smoothed by Gaussian filter $G_\sigma$, whose
standard deviation $\sigma$ controls smoothing strength. Brightness threshold
$t$ separates pixels into a binary mask $M$:
\begin{equation}
 M(x,y)=\begin{cases}1,&(G_\sigma*I)(x,y)>t,\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
 \label{eq:contour-mask}
\end{equation}
Here $(x,y)$ is pixel position and $*$ denotes convolution. This separation
is called \emph{segmentation}. The baseline uses $\sigma=1$ pixel and $t=0.5$.

ContourPy's marching-squares algorithm traces boundaries between mask values
0 and 1 at level 0.5. This fixed tracing level differs from the adjustable
image threshold $t$. Only closed curves (loops) of area at least
$A_{\min}=25$ square pixels are retained. Open curves are excluded.
Outer boundaries and boundaries around holes count separately.

\subsubsection{Calculate counts, areas, and perimeters}
A loop's $n$ ordered boundary points $(x_i,y_i)$ form a polygon. Area $A$
is calculated from adjacent coordinates. Perimeter $P$ sums segment lengths:
\begin{equation}
 A=\frac12\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i y_{i+1}-x_{i+1}y_i)\right|,
 \qquad
 P=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sqrt{(x_{i+1}-x_i)^2+(y_{i+1}-y_i)^2}.
 \label{eq:contour-geometry}
\end{equation}
The loop closes with $(x_{n+1},y_{n+1})=(x_1,y_1)$. Area uses square pixels
and perimeter pixels. The baseline retains 19,468 contours, with 0--63 per
image and median 14. Three images have none. It excludes 21,604 open curves
and 44,907 undersized or invalid closed curves.

\subsubsection{Compare blob and contour detection}
On the same 1,310 images, the baselines produce 85,074 blobs and 19,468 contours.
The pickup in Figure~\ref{fig:blob-contour-comparison} contains 106 blobs and
20 contours in the full image.

\begin{figure}[H]
 \centering
 \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{Images/blob_contour_detail_comparison.png}
 \caption{Matching crops from \texttt{pickup/PIC\_1.jpg}: LoG blobs (left) and
 closed contours (right). Dashed boxes mark common regions. Yellow/cyan circles

 \label{fig:blob-contour-comparison}
\end{figure}

LoG produces multiple circles along the windshield, while the red contour follows
the bright frame. Near the headlamp, blob circles overlap the housing and adjacent
bodywork without isolating the lamp. Contours capture only small bright patches.
Blob radii represent contrast scale, whereas contour areas measure segmented
regions, including the frame rather than the glass.

\subsubsection{Discuss advantages and limitations}
\textbf{Blob detection.} LoG provides centers and approximate sizes for bright
and dark features at several scales. Circles cannot preserve irregular outlines.
Nearby edges and background contrast can generate multiple candidates, and the
scale range limits detectable sizes.

\textbf{Contour detection.} Contours provide outlines for measuring area and
perimeter, but depend on segmentation. A threshold can omit or
fragment a component. Excluding open curves removes boundaries that intersect
the image edge, while separate inner and outer loops can inflate component counts.
Neither method identifies vehicle components or establishes accuracy without
annotated detections.

\textbf{Threshold.} Raising $t$ reduces the total from 19,468 to 14,793,
but increases counts in 296 individual images. Changing pixel membership
can split or join regions, so the effect is not uniformly decreasing.
The pickup also illustrates why counts alone are insufficient: $t=0.35$ and
$t=0.5$ both retain 14 contours but produce different boundaries.

\textbf{Smoothing.} Without smoothing, fragmented detail raises the total
to 27,079. Increasing $\sigma$ to 2 reduces it to 12,066 and increases mean area from
358.8 to 450.9 square pixels. Fine regions disappear or merge, changing both
boundary geometry and the size distribution.

\textbf{Minimum area.} Raising $A_{\min}$ from 25 to 100 leaves 8,639
contours with mean area 745.1 square pixels. This removes existing small loops
without changing the mask or surviving boundaries. Parameter selection must
reflect the required detail and target region.

\subsubsection{Give examples of suitable applications}
\textbf{Locating surface defects.} LoG can be used to locate isolated bright or
dark spots on a uniform surface, for example approximately round stains in an
inspection image. Each detection provides a center and an estimated radius,
which can support counting candidates and selecting regions for closer
inspection. Contours are preferable when the defect's full outline is needed
to measure its area.

\textbf{Measuring shape.} Contours are suitable for measuring the outline,
area, or perimeter of an isolated region or manufactured part. The windshield
frame illustrates how an elongated region can be represented more closely than
by circles. Measuring the glass would require a mask that isolates the glass
instead. A controlled, contrasting background would make part measurements
more reliable. For shape measurement, a correctly separated region matters more
than a high number of detections.
