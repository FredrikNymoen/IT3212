\subsection{Contour Detection}
\label{sec:contour-detection}

\subsubsection{Apply contour detection to the same dataset}
Contour detection was applied to ten images from the same dataset: the first
two alphabetically sorted filenames per vehicle class. All contour experiments
and the matched blob comparison use this fixed subset. Images were
EXIF-oriented, resized without enlargement to a maximum side of 512 pixels
with preserved aspect ratio, and converted to grayscale intensities in $[0,1]$.

The baseline smooths image $I$ with Gaussian filter $G_\sigma$, using
$\sigma=1$ pixel, then thresholds brightness at $t=0.5$:
\begin{equation}
 M(x,y)=\begin{cases}1,&(G_\sigma*I)(x,y)>t,\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
 \label{eq:contour-mask}
\end{equation}
ContourPy's marching-squares engine traces the binary mask's 0.5 level.
The implementation retains closed loops of area $A\geq25$ square pixels;
open paths are excluded rather than closed across the frame. Outer and hole
boundaries are counted separately, without constructing an object hierarchy.

\subsubsection{Visualize contours using different colors}
Figure~\ref{fig:contour-example} shows the mask and retained boundaries.
Each loop has a distinct color and measurement ID; colors do not encode
vehicle classes. The rear-window region has an irregular boundary, while
dark car and background regions merge in the mask. Thus, the output follows

\subsubsection{Calculate counts, areas, and perimeters}
For ordered loop vertices $(x_i,y_i)$, with vertex $n+1$ equal to vertex 1,
area $A$ and perimeter $P$ are
\begin{equation}
 A=\frac12\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i y_{i+1}-x_{i+1}y_i)\right|,
 \qquad
 P=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sqrt{(x_{i+1}-x_i)^2+(y_{i+1}-y_i)^2}.
 \label{eq:contour-geometry}
\end{equation}
Measurements use processed pixels. The baseline retains 158 contours and
excludes 166 open paths and 436 small or degenerate closed paths.
Table~\ref{tab:detection-image-stats} reports every analyzed image; individual
measurements and vertices are exported separately. Hole and outer-loop areas
overlap, so summing them would not give net foreground area.

\subsubsection{Compare blob and contour detection}
Recomputing the blob baseline on these same ten images gives 695 blobs,
compared with 158 contours. The pickup has 110 blobs and 14 contours:
repeated local responses along window and bumper details can correspond to
only a few elongated region boundaries. The higher blob count does not
indicate greater accuracy. Blob radius estimates contrast scale; contour
area measures an enclosed polygon, so their sizes are not interchangeable.

\subsubsection{Discuss advantages and limitations}
LoG provides compact center-and-scale features for both bright and dark
regions, but circles do not preserve irregular shape and can respond repeatedly
to edges and reflections. Contours preserve boundary geometry and support
area and perimeter measurements, but depend on segmentation: global
thresholding can merge unrelated regions or fragment one object. Excluding
open paths also removes frame-intersecting boundaries. Neither method
recognizes vehicles from these measurements. Without detection annotations,
precision and recall cannot be calculated; the ten-image comparison also
cannot establish full-dataset performance.

\textbf{Threshold.} Sample totals decrease as brightness threshold rises,
but the pickup retains 14 loops at both $t=0.35$ and $t=0.5$, with different
window and bumper boundaries. At $t=0.65$, the broad window region becomes
a narrow highlight. Equal counts therefore conceal changes in shape and
position; connectivity changes also make counts non-monotonic in general.

\textbf{Smoothing.} Without smoothing, fragmented detail raises the total
to 230 and mean perimeter to 101.8 pixels. Increasing $\sigma$ to 2 reduces
the total to 96 and mean perimeter to 88.1 pixels. Smoothing suppresses fine
structure but may also merge regions, so it changes more than boundary roughness.

\textbf{Minimum area.} Raising $A_{\min}$ from 25 to 100 reduces the total
to 65 and increases mean retained area to 773.4 square pixels. This filters
existing loops without changing the mask or surviving boundaries; it does
not improve segmentation. Parameter selection therefore depends on the
required detail and target region, rather than minimizing detection counts.

\subsubsection{Give examples of suitable applications}
LoG is suitable for locating candidate lamp details or reflective spots when
position and approximate scale are required. Contours support measurements
of irregular regions, such as the sedan window, if segmentation isolates the
boundary. Silhouette measurements require a contrasting background; shadows
and reflections limit this application in the present photographs.
