\subsection{Blob Detection}
\label{sec:blob-detection}

\subsubsection{Apply blob detection to the dataset}
A multiscale Laplacian-of-Gaussian (LoG) detector was applied to all 1,310
images. Images were EXIF-oriented, resized without enlargement to a maximum
side of 512 pixels while preserving aspect ratio, and converted to grayscale
intensities in $[0,1]$. All measurements use processed-image pixels.

For image $I$, the scale-normalized response is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{R_\sigma(x,y)=-\sigma^2\nabla^2(G_\sigma*I)(x,y)}.
 \label{eq:blob-log}
\end{equation}
Here $(x,y)$ denotes pixel position, $G_\sigma$ a Gaussian filter of standard
deviation $\sigma$, $*$ convolution, and $\nabla^2$ the sum of second spatial
derivatives. Small scales examine fine detail; large scales examine broader
regions. The factor $\sigma^2$ normalizes responses across scales, and the
minus sign makes bright centers positive and dark centers negative.

The implementation uses SciPy derivatives and identifies maxima of
$|R_\sigma|$ in a $3\times3\times3$ spatial-scale neighborhood. Candidates
above response threshold $\tau$ receive radius $r=\sqrt{2}\sigma$, an
approximate feature scale. Frame-crossing circles are excluded; remaining
candidates are processed strongest first, suppressing circles whose
intersection exceeds a specified fraction of the smaller circle's area.

The baseline uses 12 geometrically spaced scales from $\sigma_{\min}=4$ to
$\sigma_{\max}=16$, $\tau=0.15$, and overlap limit 0.5. These parameters control
feature size, response strength, and duplicate suppression, respectively.
Additional pre-smoothing is disabled because LoG already includes Gaussian
smoothing. This configuration provides the reference for the experiments below.

\subsubsection{Visualize the detected blobs}
Ten fixed display examples were chosen as the first two alphabetically sorted
filenames from each of the five classes. This reproducible selection covers
every class but is not a representative sample; all 1,310 images remain in
the numerical analysis. Figure~\ref{fig:blob-example} uses two of these
examples to contrast repeated pickup details with broader sedan regions.

Repeated responses occur along the pickup window and bumper, while a broad
dark response covers part of the sedan rear window. Reflections and background
regions also respond: the circles locate contrast features, not vehicle outlines.

\subsubsection{Evaluate the effect of algorithm parameters}
Six variants each change one baseline parameter on all 1,310 images
(Table~\ref{tab:blob-parameters}). Figure~\ref{fig:blob-parameters} holds the
pickup and sedan fixed to show which structures survive different thresholds.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\small
\caption{Parameter sensitivity across all 1,310 images. Each variant changes one parameter; mean radius is calculated over individual blobs (pixels).}
\label{tab:blob-parameters}
\begin{tabular}{llrr}
\toprule
Setting & Value & Total blobs & Mean radius \\
\midrule
Baseline & $\tau=0.15,\ \sigma=4$--$16$ & 85,074 & 7.40 \\
Lower threshold & $\tau=0.075$ & 198,896 & 6.88 \\
Higher threshold & $\tau=0.225$ & 25,854 & 7.86 \\
Larger minimum & $\sigma_{\min}=7$ & 35,726 & 11.93 \\
Larger maximum & $\sigma_{\max}=30$ & 84,213 & 7.60 \\
Stricter overlap & Overlap limit $=0.2$ & 77,302 & 7.36 \\
Extra smoothing & Pre-smoothing $\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}=2$ & 44,825 & 9.42 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\begin{figure}[H]
 \centering
 \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{Images/blob_report_thresholds.png}
 \caption{Pickup (top) and sedan (bottom) at increasing response thresholds;
 other parameters remain at baseline. Counts refer to each displayed image.}
 \label{fig:blob-parameters}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Threshold.} Lowering $\tau$ increases counts in every image; raising
it decreases counts in every image. At the high threshold, repeated pickup
window-edge responses remain, but the broad dark sedan window response
disappears while bright reflections remain. A larger blob therefore need not
have a stronger response. The increase in aggregate mean radius does not
imply that each surviving feature is larger.

\textbf{Scale range.} Raising $\sigma_{\min}$ to 7 excludes radii below
9.90 pixels, removing fine features and increasing mean radius. Raising
$\sigma_{\max}$ to 30 permits broader features but slightly reduces the total.
With 12 scales fixed, changing an endpoint also changes the intermediate
sampling grid; this is not simply adding larger scales to the baseline.

\textbf{Overlap and smoothing.} A stricter overlap limit removes more
competing circles, potentially including nearby distinct features.
Pre-smoothing with $\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}=2$ suppresses fine detail, reducing
counts and increasing mean radius. These changes show sensitivity, not
improved accuracy: vehicle class labels provide no blob annotations with
which to identify an optimal setting.
