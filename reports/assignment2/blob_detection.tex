\subsection{Blob Detection}
\label{sec:blob-detection}

\subsubsection{Apply blob detection to the dataset}
A blob is a region brighter or darker than its surroundings. A
Laplacian-of-Gaussian (LoG) detector located blob centers and sizes in all
1,310 images. Images were oriented using camera metadata, resized without
enlargement to a maximum side of 512 pixels while preserving aspect ratio,
and converted to grayscale values in $[0,1]$. Measurements use prepared-image pixels.

LoG smooths image $I$ with Gaussian filter $G_\sigma$, then measures intensity
curvature at position $(x,y)$:
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{R_\sigma(x,y)=-\sigma^2\nabla^2(G_\sigma*I)(x,y)}.
 \label{eq:blob-log}
\end{equation}
Here $\sigma$ is the filter's standard deviation in pixels, $*$ means
convolution, and $\nabla^2$ sums second derivatives along both image axes.
Small $\sigma$ examines fine detail; larger $\sigma$ examines broader regions.
The factor $\sigma^2$ makes responses comparable across scales. Positive
responses indicate bright centers and negative responses dark centers,
called their \emph{polarity}.

Using SciPy filters, the detector finds peaks in magnitude $|R_\sigma|$
within a $3\times3$ pixel window across three scale levels. Peaks above
threshold $\tau$ receive estimated radius $r=\sqrt{2}\sigma$.
Frame-crossing circles are excluded. Candidates are retained strongest first;
a weaker circle is rejected when its intersection with a retained circle
exceeds the overlap limit, measured relative to the smaller circle's area.

The reference configuration, or \emph{baseline}, uses 12 scales with a
constant ratio between $\sigma_{\min}=4$ and $\sigma_{\max}=16$,
response threshold $\tau=0.15$, and overlap limit 0.5.

\subsubsection{Evaluate the effect of algorithm parameters}
Six variants each change one parameter on all 1,310 images
(Table~\ref{tab:blob-parameters}). Extra Gaussian smoothing before LoG uses
standard deviation $\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}$, set to zero in the baseline.
Figure~\ref{fig:blob-parameters} holds both example images fixed across thresholds.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\small
\caption{Parameter sensitivity across all 1,310 images. Each variant changes one parameter; mean radius is calculated over individual blobs (pixels).}
\label{tab:blob-parameters}
\begin{tabular}{llrr}
\toprule
Setting & Value & Total blobs & Mean radius \\
\midrule
Baseline & $\tau=0.15,\ \sigma=4$--$16$ & 85,074 & 7.40 \\

\bottomrule

\begin{figure}[H]
 \centering
 \includegraphics[width=0.92\linewidth]{Images/blob_report_thresholds.png}
 \caption{Pickup (top) and sedan (bottom) at increasing response thresholds;
 other parameters remain at baseline. Counts refer to each displayed image.}
 \label{fig:blob-parameters}

\textbf{Threshold.} Lowering $\tau$ increases counts in every image; raising
it decreases counts in every image. At the high threshold, repeated pickup
window-edge responses remain, but the broad dark sedan window response
disappears while bright reflections remain. Blob size therefore does not determine response strength.

\textbf{Scale range.} Raising $\sigma_{\min}$ to 7 excludes radii below
9.90 pixels, removing fine features and increasing mean radius. Raising
$\sigma_{\max}$ to 30 permits broader features but slightly reduces the total.
With 12 scales fixed, changing either limit also changes the intermediate
scales, so existing detections can disappear.

\textbf{Overlap and smoothing.} A stricter overlap limit removes more
competing circles, potentially including nearby distinct features.
Pre-smoothing with $\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}=2$ suppresses fine detail, reducing
counts and increasing mean radius. These changes show sensitivity, not
improved accuracy: vehicle class labels provide no blob annotations with
which to identify an optimal setting.
