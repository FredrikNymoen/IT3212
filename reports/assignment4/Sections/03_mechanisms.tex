\subsection{Controlled one-factor comparisons}
In the same Ryzen~5 7500F / PyTorch~2.15.0 environment and Setting~B protocol (262,144 steps,
five seeds, study \texttt{e8c0c6a8f4071325}), we varied one mechanism at a time across three
values with all other settings fixed. All Q sweeps and PPO's $\gamma$, entropy and learning-rate
sweeps branched from the Part~2 baseline; only PPO's epoch sweep carried forward $2\times$ LR
(Table~\ref{tab:part3-sweeps}, Figure~\ref{fig:mechanisms}).

\subsection{Diagnostics and prediction checks}
\paragraph{Early-learning diagnostic (\texttt{3142fac3111db314}).}
At 65,536 steps, dev.\ success was 10\% (Q) vs.\ 0\% (6-epoch PPO, $2\times$ LR) and 20\%
(12-epoch PPO), with seed~17 favouring Q. To test whether 10-episode noise explained the
reversal, we froze all 15 checkpoints at 65,536 steps on 50 fresh seeds (1370000--1370049):
Q averaged 5.6\%, 6-epoch PPO 0.0\%, and 12-epoch PPO 29.6\% (beating 6-epoch PPO on 5/5
seeds and Q on 4/5; seed~17 stayed 8\% vs.\ 2\%). Thus Q's early lead depends on PPO's rollout reuse.

\paragraph{Zero-entropy execution diagnostic (\texttt{exec\_diagnostic}).}
Because zero-entropy PPO led baseline at the 262k cap (64\% vs.\ 36\% dev.), we tested whether
argmax concentration rather than a generally stronger distribution drove the gain. On 50 fresh
paired seeds (1700000--1700049, Table~\ref{tab:diagnostic}), the gap narrowed from $+20.0$\,pp
under argmax to $+14.0$\,pp under sampling, while visited-state entropy dropped ($0.624\to 0.426$ nats).
Execution mode amplifies the cap gain, though a sampling advantage remains (seed~2718 reversed under argmax, 48\% vs.\ 74\%).

\paragraph{Training return and same-state probes.}
Training-return ranks correlated positively with eval.\ success in 5/6 A/B/C cohorts (negative
in B~PPO, $\rho=-0.10$); training leaders led eval.\ in only 3/6. Same-state probes on 6,306 B
states left State~1 inconclusive ($n=6$), State~2 unverifiable (capped RAM~116 proxy), and
contradicted State~3 ($n=109$: PPO chose RIGHT 98.7\%, LEFT $<1\%$).
